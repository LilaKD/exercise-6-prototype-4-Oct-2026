Partnership Architecture Workshop

The Partnership Architecture Workshop establishes whether partnerships are a growth channel worth building for you, and which partners to approach.

Typical timeframe: 4 to 6 weeks

The Partnership Architecture Workshop establishes whether partnerships are a growth channel worth building for a business, and which partners it should approach.

Typical timeframe: 4 to 6 weeks
When marketing is not bringing you the right clients

Use this work when traditional marketing is producing too few of the right clients, or when referrals matter to you but arrive inconsistently.

The workshop tests whether partnerships could close that gap, what they could produce for you, how long it may take and which relationships are worth pursuing.

You know whether the channel is worth investing in, and why.

Where this work fits, and where it does not

A strong fit

Established businesses in financial and professional services.

  • You already refer clients to trusted specialists.
  • You know complementary providers serving the same clients.
  • You can show a prospective partner a credible track record.

A weaker fit

Businesses less likely to benefit from this work.

  • Too new to demonstrate a track record.
  • Rarely needing to refer clients elsewhere.
  • Not set up to collaborate with other providers.
What you hold at the end of it
A decision you can stand behindWhether partnerships are the right growth channel for your business, and the evidence the answer rests on.
A realistic number, and a realistic timeframeThe revenue partnerships can generate for you, and how long it takes to reach it.
A shortlist worth workingWhich partners to approach, why each one is on the list, and what the relationship would ask of your team.
An agreed picture of successWhat success looks like, and how you will know the channel is working.

If the first conversation confirms a fit, the next step is a Partner Opportunity Session. We will arrange a time that suits your team.

What it helps with
  • The workshop helps businesses work out whether partnerships are right for them, what revenue they can realistically generate from them, how long it will take, what effort is involved, which partners to approach and why, and what success looks like, so they can pursue a partnership strategy with clarity about outcomes and benefits.
  • It helps businesses turn referral partnerships into a consistent growth channel, replacing marketing that is not working with a better quality of leads.

This is the whole of it in two sentences. If a client of yours would be better off for hearing it, the rest of this reading tells you when and how.

Who it's for
  • It helps established businesses in financial and professional services that already refer clients out, have overlapping service providers in their networks, and can demonstrate a track record to prospective partners.

Where the fit is weaker

  • Businesses that are less likely to benefit include those just starting out without social proof, those that provide all services in-house and have little need to refer clients elsewhere, and those that do not have a collaborative mindset.

Fit is the first thing to check. An introduction into a poor fit costs you more than the one you did not make.

A partner is likely to hear things like

Our marketing just isn't working

We're getting tyre kickers, not the right clients

The audience we need doesn't respond to marketing

My referrals all depend on me and I want a more consistent way to grow

We need to grow but I don't want lead generation relying on one person

The moments that matter
  • A specific trigger is often a personnel change in the person responsible for referrals or networking, a growth target with a deadline, or a business setting its marketing budget or direction for the coming year.
  • It also becomes pressing when a business is opening a new service area and needs partners to open doors to those markets.

When it becomes relevant

  • It becomes relevant when marketing is not producing the right results, lead quality or conversion is low, the target audience does not respond to traditional marketing, or the business needs a more consistent and systematic way to grow.
How it works
  • Discover and Qualify. A short conversation about how the business grows today and whether the work is worth their time. Nothing is asked of them before that.
  • Partner Opportunity Session. The bridge step. The opportunity is sized with them: which partnerships are realistic, what they could be worth, and what they would ask of the business.
  • Partner Plan Workshop. The strategy is written down: the partners to approach and why, the revenue to expect, the time it will take and what success looks like.
  • Partner Suitcase and emPartner. The material and the system their team uses to run the strategy, so the approach is held by the business rather than by one person.
  • Partner Circle. Continuing support once the plan is in their hands, so the partnerships keep moving after the workshop.
  • Fractional Partnership Management. Where the business would rather the work was carried for it, the partnerships are managed alongside their team.

Your client hears these stage names in the same order you do, so nothing you say here is contradicted later.

When an introduction would help most

Any sign of struggle with sales, marketing or lead quality is the moment to introduce the partnership approach, and the workshop can be valuable even before a business raises those issues directly, as most established businesses would benefit from knowing that referral partnerships can be systemised.

How the introduction is made

  • Raise the workshop in the conversation where the issue surfaced, and offer to make the connection.
  • Send Emple Australia the business name and a line about what prompted it. You will hear back after the first conversation, and you will know what was agreed.

Accountants, the worked archetype, from Exercise 3

Why you matter to them

Clients ask them for growth advice they are not resourced to give.

Why they matter to you

They sit across the finances of established firms and hear the growth question early.

Triggers they encounter

Marketing that is not producing the right results, low lead quality or conversion, an audience that does not respond to traditional marketing, and a business that needs a more consistent and systematic way to grow.

What those moments cost the client

Loss of revenue, an enormous amount of stress, loss of time, effort and energy, and an inability to realise their vision.

How often they see it

In most annual and quarterly reviews, and reliably at budget setting.

How close they are to the client

Long standing and trusted. The relationship often predates every other adviser.

Can they act on it

Yes. A warm introduction sits inside the review conversation they already hold.
